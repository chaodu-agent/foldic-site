FAQ

Do I have to buy Foldic twice for Mac and iPhone?

No. Foldic is a universal purchase: buy it once and it works on both, on any device signed in to your Apple Account. There is no subscription and nothing to unlock inside the app.

What does the iPhone version need?

An iPhone with a USB-C port and a drive it can read — exFAT, FAT32 and APFS all work. A bus-powered NVMe SSD is the natural pairing: fast enough that the copy is limited by your photos, not the disk. A spinning drive may need its own power supply. You can also copy to anywhere the Files app can reach, but an attached drive is the point of it.

Does the iPhone version need internet or iCloud?

Neither, and this is the point of the iPhone version. No iCloud subscription, no account, no connection: photos move from your device straight to the attached drive, and nothing is uploaded to any service, Foldic's or Apple's. You can do it on a plane. If you have deliberately stayed off iCloud, this is the way to get every album out of Photos and onto a disk you own.

The one exception is Optimized Storage. If your library keeps full-resolution originals in iCloud rather than on the phone, those have to be fetched before they can be written, and that needs a connection. Photos already on the device are copied offline.

Why does copying stop when I leave the app on iPhone?

iOS does not let an app keep an external drive open once it is off screen, so Foldic keeps the screen awake and asks you to leave it in front while a copy runs. Nothing is lost if you do switch away: copying is incremental, and starting it again continues where it stopped. A large library can be done across several sittings.

Does Foldic modify my Photos library?

No. Foldic is strictly one-way: it reads your albums through Apple's PhotoKit and writes files to a folder you choose. It never edits, deletes, or reorganizes anything in Photos.

Where do my photos go?

To a folder you pick — on your Mac, an external drive, or a NAS mount. Each album becomes its own subfolder of original-quality files.

What about Live Photos?

Both parts are exported: the still image and the paired video file.

How fast do iPhone photos show up?

As soon as iCloud syncs them to your Mac. Keeping Photos.app running makes iCloud deliver faster; Foldic also re-checks your library every few minutes.

How do I back up an iCloud Shared Album?

Shared Albums are invitation-based albums that other people can view or contribute to. They are different from an iCloud Shared Library. First, make sure the Shared Album appears and has finished syncing in Photos on your Mac. Foldic labels it shared; sync it like any other album to export every item you can currently access into its own folder.

Foldic is one-way: it never posts, edits, or restores content in a Shared Album. Shared Albums do not necessarily expose the original uploader’s full-quality file, so Foldic exports the highest-quality version that Apple Photos makes available to you.

If an owner removes your access, deletes the Shared Album, or removes an item from it, Foldic marks the affected local exports as orphaned after its next scan. It never deletes those files automatically.

Does Foldic back up Smart Albums?

Smart Albums are live, rule-based views in Apple Photos, such as Favorites, Videos, or albums you create with rules. Foldic currently backs up regular albums, iCloud Shared Albums, and All Photos. It does not yet mirror other Smart Albums as separate folders.

To ensure those photos are backed up today, sync All Photos, or add the photos you want to preserve as a separate mirror to a regular album.

What happens if I remove or delete a photo in Apple Photos?

It depends on where you remove it. If you remove a photo from an album, it remains in your Photos Library and in any other albums. After Foldic scans again, only the exported copy in that album’s folder is marked as orphaned.

If you delete a photo from your Photos Library, Apple moves it to Recently Deleted and removes that photo from every album that contains it. After Foldic scans again, all affected exported copies — including copies in album folders and, if enabled, the All Photos mirror — are marked as orphaned.

Foldic never deletes exported files automatically. You decide whether to keep or remove orphaned files.
